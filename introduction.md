# Introduction: Preparing for an IB Computer Science Teaching Role

This project documents a practical preparation plan for becoming qualified and credible to teach the **IB Diploma Programme (DP) Computer Science** course.

## Goal

The goal is not to claim existing IB Computer Science expertise, but to build:

- Strong subject knowledge
- Familiarity with IB course requirements
- Experience using IB-aligned teaching and assessment methods
- A clear, honest presentation for the teacher interview

## Course awareness

Candidates should confirm with the school which course guide and exam session they use. The current DP Computer Science guide introduced for first teaching in August 2025 and first assessment in May 2027 is organised around:

### Theme A: Concepts of Computer Science

- Computer fundamentals
- Networks
- Databases
- Machine Learning

### Theme B: Computational Thinking and Problem-solving

- Computational thinking
- Programming
- Object-oriented programming
- Abstract Data Types for Higher Level

Both Standard Level and Higher Level include the pre-seen case study. The school may teach the programming component in Java or Python.

## Assessment awareness

Prepare to discuss:

- **Paper 1:** theoretical concepts and the case study
- **Paper 2:** algorithmic thinking, programming, and problem-solving
- **Internal Assessment:** an individual open-ended computational solution developed for a real client
- **Collaborative Sciences Project:** a group project required across Group 4 subjects

Internal Assessment work should demonstrate planning, design, development, testing, and evaluation. Teachers must guide students without completing the work or code for them.

## Six-week preparation plan

### Week 1: Understand the role and identify gaps

- Review the job description and identify whether the role covers SL, HL, or both.
- Ask which programming language and exam session the school uses.
- Read the official subject guide and assessment materials.
- Create a syllabus checklist and mark every topic as:
  - Confident
  - Needs practice
  - New

**Deliverable:** a one-page course map and personal learning checklist.

### Weeks 2–3: Build core computer science knowledge

Study and practise:

- Computational thinking, algorithms, tracing, and pseudocode
- Variables, conditionals, loops, functions, collections, testing, and debugging
- Classes, objects, encapsulation, inheritance, and polymorphism
- Data representation and computer systems
- Networks
- Databases
- Machine learning concepts

Writing and explaining small programs is more useful than only watching demonstrations.

**Deliverable:** working examples and short explanations suitable for beginners.

### Week 4: Learn assessment and teaching expectations

- Review specimen assessment materials and mark schemes.
- Practise solving and explaining exam-style problems.
- Understand the Internal Assessment criteria and development process.
- Plan a formative assessment that identifies one likely misconception and a suitable response.

**Deliverable:** a lesson outline and an assessment or check-for-understanding activity.

### Week 5: Prepare an interview lesson

Prepare a 10–15 minute lesson on a topic you can teach confidently. Suitable choices include:

- Tracing an algorithm
- Debugging a loop
- Introducing object-oriented programming
- Explaining a database concept
- Using a network or data representation example

Include:

1. A clear learning objective
2. A real-world problem or hook
3. An activity that prompts reasoning
4. A concise worked example
5. A check for understanding
6. A short reflection or next step

Practise it aloud and adapt it for students with different levels of prior experience.

### Week 6: Rehearse the interview

Prepare concise answers to questions about:

- Why you want to teach IB Computer Science
- Supporting students without prior programming experience
- Making abstract concepts understandable
- Identifying and addressing misconceptions
- Guiding Internal Assessment work without completing it for students
- Responding honestly when you do not know a technical answer
- Supporting learners who need additional challenge or scaffolding
- Using inquiry, reflection, and real-world contexts

## Interview evidence

Bring or be ready to discuss:

- A one-page course map
- A sample lesson plan
- A small programming or algorithm example
- A formative assessment task
- A clear professional-development plan for areas still needing improvement

## How to present experience

Do not claim IB teaching experience or subject mastery that you do not have. Instead:

- State clearly what you already know
- Describe exactly how you are addressing gaps
- Give specific examples from teaching or software experience
- Explain how you would learn from students and colleagues
- Demonstrate readiness to complete relevant IB professional development

## Completion criteria

The preparation is complete when you can:

- Explain the course structure and assessment process accurately
- Teach a short, engaging SL-level lesson confidently
- Solve and explain basic programming and algorithm problems
- Discuss the SL/HL distinction
- Explain the role of the case study
- Demonstrate ethical and useful Internal Assessment support
- Identify your remaining knowledge gaps and have a plan to address them
